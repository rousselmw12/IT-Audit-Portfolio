Objective & Methodology

Objective & Methodology

Tested 12 system changes deployed during Q3 2025 across MedCore's clinical and administrative platforms. For each change, verified two independent requirements: (1) formal approval was obtained before deployment, and (2) post-deployment testing was completed. A change was flagged as an exception if either condition was missing — using an OR logic flag so partial compliance is still caught.

Key Findings
  • 4 changes deployed without proper approval (CHG-303, CHG-305, CHG-308, CHG-311)
  • 5 changes deployed without post-deployment testing (CHG-302, CHG-306, CHG-308, CHG-310, CHG-311)
  • CHG-308 (Billing System) and CHG-302 (Billing System) are the highest-risk findings — Billing handles patient financial data
  • 5 changes passed all requirements with no exceptions (CHG-301, CHG-304, CHG-307, CHG-309, CHG-312)

Objective & Methodology

Reviewed backup job logs for all 12 servers across MedCore's clinical and infrastructure environment over Q3 2025 (92 job cycles per server). For each server, calculated the failure rate and verified whether all failures were investigated and resolved. A server was flagged if it had backup failures that were not investigated — regardless of failure rate — since any unresolved failure creates data recovery risk.

Key Findings
  • SRV-BCK-01 (Backup Controller): 12 failures, 13.04% failure rate — not investigated. Highest risk as this is the backup infrastructure itself.
  • SRV-BIL-01 (Billing): 7 failures at 7.61% — not investigated. Billing data recovery is at risk.
  • SRV-PHR-02 (Pharmacy): 5 failures at 5.43% — not investigated. Last successful backup: Sep 25 (5 days before quarter end).
  • SRV-RAD-02 (Radiology): 4 failures at 4.35% — not investigated.
  • 4 servers had zero failures; 4 servers with failures properly investigated — no exception.

Objective & Methodology

Tested all 8 system deployments (3 new systems + 5 major upgrades) completed in Q3 2025 against MedCore IT Policy IT-POL-012. The policy requires every deployment to complete unit testing, user acceptance testing (UAT), security review, business owner approval, and IT security approval before go-live. Tested 100% of the population. Given the healthcare context and patient data exposure, the tolerable deviation rate was set at 0% — any gap is an exception.

Key Findings
  • 5 of 8 deployments had exceptions — all 5 involved systems that process Protected Health Information (PHI)
  • Billing System Upgrade & Radiology PACS: both testing and approval were incomplete before go-live
  • Pharmacy Management v3 & Lab Results System: deployed without completing UAT
  • EHR System Upgrade: all testing passed but deployed without business or IT security approval
  • Non-PHI systems (HR Self-Service, Backup Software Upgrade) had no exceptions

Objective & Methodology

Tested MedCore's quarterly access recertification process per IT Policy IT-POL-008 and HIPAA requirements. Managers are required to review and certify access for all users each quarter, and any required access changes must be submitted within 5 business days. Tested all 15 active clinical and administrative staff for Q3 2026. Exceptions were flagged for two failure types: review not completed (access never certified) and access change required but never submitted.

Key Findings
  • 4 users had access never reviewed or certified this quarter: Samuel Park (Radiology), Elena Vasquez (Nursing), Ingrid Larsen (Radiology), Lydia Osei (IT)
  • 3 users had required access changes that were never submitted: Fatima Hassan (Pharmacy), Diana Owusu (Nursing), Chloe Asante (Pharmacy)
  • Radiology and Nursing had the most exceptions — departments with direct PHI access
  • 8 users had no exceptions — reviews completed and any required changes properly submitted
